# Business Profits Tax Regulations

BUSINESS PROFITS TAX REGULATIONS

## PART I

Preliminary § 101. Short Title These Regulations may be cited as the Business Profits Tax Regulations. § 102. Authority These Regulations are promulgated pursuant to Section 1472 of the Act. § 103. Definitions

(a) Unless the context requires otherwise, for the purpose of these Regulations:

(1) “Act” means the Business Profits Act, Chapter 14 of Title 40.

(2) “Bank” means a “bank” as defined in Section 1002 of Title 26 that is licensed to operate in the Republic.

(3) “Commencement date” means 1st January 2023.

(4) “Cross-border transaction” means a transaction between:

(A) a resident and a non-resident except where the transaction takes place wholly in the Republic;

(B) two residents where the transaction relates to a business carried on through a permanent establishment outside the Republic by one or both residents; or

(C) two non-residents where the transaction relates to a business carried on through a permanent establishment in the Republic by one of the non- residents.

(5) Reserved

(6) “General insurance business” means the business of undertaking liability, by way of insurance (including reinsurance), in respect of any loss or damage, including liability to pay damages or compensation, contingent upon the happening of a specified event, other than a life insurance business.

(7) “Instalment period”, in relation to a taxpayer for a tax year, means the period of three (3) months ending on the last day of the third, sixth, ninth, and twelfth months of the year.

(8) “Intangible asset” means:

(A) a copyright, patent, design or model, plan, secret formula or process, trademark, or other like property or right that has a limited useful life and is used wholly or partly to derive gross revenue;

(B) contractual rights (including arising as a result of a prepayment of expenses) with a limited term, but which exceeds one (1) year, used wholly or partly to derive gross revenue; or

(C) preliminary expenditure.

(9) “Life insurance business” means the business of issuing a contract of insurance that provides for the payment of money on the death of a person or on the happening of a contingency dependent on the termination or continuance of human life.

(10) “Membership interest”, in relation to an entity, means a share in a company, an interest in a partnership or trust, or any other ownership interest in an entity.

(11) “Option” means an agreement under which a person has a right to purchase an asset from, or a right to sell an asset to, another person within an agreed period of time for a specified or determinable price.

(12) “Preliminary expenditure” means expenditure incurred by a person in establishing a business but before the commencement of business operations, but only where the business is to be carried on wholly and exclusively to derive gross revenue, other than expenditure incurred to acquire tangible personal, or real property, or an intangible asset within paragraphs (A) or (B) of the definition of “intangible asset”.

(13) “Property income” means:

(A) a dividend, interest; royalty; rent; or annuity; or

(B) a gain on disposal of an asset giving rise to income referred to in Sub- paragraph (A).

(14) “Straight-line depreciation” means the calculation of the amount of the depreciation deduction allowed under Section 1002(y)(10) of Title 40 for a depreciable asset by applying the straight-line rate specified in Regulation 202 against the cost of the asset in accordance with these Regulations.

(15) “Transfer pricing arrangement” means a transaction, or series of transactions, that satisfies all the following conditions:

(A) the transaction is for the supply or acquisition of goods, services, money, intangibles, or an asset;

(B) the transaction is between persons who are associates; and

(C) the transaction is a cross-border transaction.

(b) A term used in these Regulations that is defined in Title 40 has the meaning in Title 40 unless the context requires otherwise.

## PART II

Depreciation § 201. Depreciation of depreciable assets

(a) Subject to Sub-regulation (b), a taxpayer may determine the depreciation deduction referred to in Section 1002(y)(10) of Title 40 according to the straight-line method.

(b) RESERVED

(c) The depreciation deduction for a depreciable asset for a tax year is calculated by reference to the rate of depreciation specified for the asset in Regulation 202.

(d) If a taxpayer uses a depreciable asset in a tax year partly to derive gross revenue and partly for another use, the amount allowed as a deduction under Section 1002(y)(10) of Title 40 is based on the proportion of the use of the asset that relates to the derivation of gross revenue.

(e) If a depreciable asset is not used by a taxpayer for the whole of a tax year to derive gross revenue, the amount allowed as a deduction under Section 1002(y)(10) of Title 40, after taking account of Sub-regulation (d), is based on the proportion of the year that the taxpayer used the asset to derive gross revenue.

(f) The total deductions allowed, or that would be allowed but for Sub-regulation (d), to a taxpayer in respect of a depreciable asset for the current tax year and all previous tax years must not exceed the cost of the asset.

(g) If the net book value of a depreciable asset of a taxpayer at the end of a tax year is less than [$300]:

(1) the undepreciated amount of the net book value of the asset is added to the taxpayer’s depreciation deduction for that year but only to the extent that the asset is used to derive gross revenue; and

(2) the asset is treated as fully depreciated. § 202. Depreciation Rates

## Part III

Banks and Insurance Companies § 301. Provision for unexpired risks

(a) A resident company carrying on a general insurance business is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the balance of the company’s provision for unexpired risks as at the end of the year. The deduction is limited to insured risks in the Republic.

(b) A non-resident company carrying on a general insurance business through a permanent establishment in the Republic is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the balance of the company’s provision for unexpired risks as at the end of the tax year. The deduction is limited to insured risks in the Republic accepted by the permanent establishment.

(c) The amount of the deduction must not exceed the amount required for the provision under GAAP.

(d) A resident or non-resident company carrying on a general insurance business for a tax year must include in the gross revenue of the company for the year the amount allowed as a deduction to the company under Sub-regulation (a) or (b) in the prior tax year. § 302. Taxation of life insurance companies

(a) A resident company carrying on a life insurance business (referred to as a “life company”) is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the following amounts:

(1) the amount of the initial reserves established in the financial accounts of the company for new life policies issued during the year for individuals in the Republic, but the amount of the deduction allowed must not exceed the amount required for the initial reserve under GAAP; and

(2) the amount of the annual additions made in the financial accounts of the company during the year to life policy reserves relating to individuals in the Republic, but the amount of the deduction allowed must not exceed the amount required for the annual addition to the reserve under GAAP.

(b) Where, during a tax year, a life company cancels a life policy issued by the company in respect of which a deduction has been allowed under Sub-regulation (a), the amount of the deducted reserves in relation to the cancelled policy is included in the gross revenue of the company for the year.

(c) Where a life company makes claim pay-outs under life policies relating to individuals in the Republic during a tax year:

(1) if the total amount of deducted reserves in relation to those claim pay-outs exceeds the total amount of claim pay-outs made during the year, the excess is included in the gross revenue of the company for the year; or

(2) if the total amount of claim pay-outs made during the year exceeds the total amount of deducted reserves in relation to those claim pay-outs, the company is allowed a deduction for the year for the amount of the excess. § 303. Loan loss provision of banks

(a) A bank is allowed a deduction for a tax year for an amount equal to the addition to the loan loss provision of the bank for the year, provided it is calculated in accordance with GAAP.

(b) The deduction under Sub-regulation (a) is limited to the addition to the loan loss provision relating to banking operations carried on in the Republic.

## Part IV

Rules Relating to Assets § 401. Jointly owned assets

(a) For the purposes of the Act, where an asset is jointly owned by two or more persons (other than in partnership or under a trust), any income, gains, expenditures, and losses relating to the asset are apportioned among the owners in accordance with their respective interests in the asset.

(b) Where the interests of the owners of a jointly-owned asset cannot be ascertained, the owners of the asset are treated as having an equal interest in the asset. § 402. Acquisition of an asset

(a) Subject to the Act and this Part, a taxpayer acquires an asset at the time that legal title to the asset is transferred to the taxpayer.

(b) A taxpayer acquires a right at the time that the right is granted to the taxpayer.

(c) Where a taxpayer constructs or creates, or arranges for the construction or creation of, an asset that the taxpayer owns when the asset is constructed or created, the taxpayer acquires the asset when the construction or the work that resulted in the creation of the asset commences. § 403. Disposal of an asset

(a) This Regulation provides for the disposal of an asset in addition to the rules in the Act.

(b) An asset transmitted by succession or under a will is treated as disposed of by the deceased at the time the asset is transmitted.

(c) A disposal includes the disposal of a part of an asset.

(d) The vesting of an asset of a taxpayer (referred to as the “owner”) in an appointed person referred to in Section 1612 of Title 40 is not a disposal of the asset for the purposes of the Act, and any acts done in relation to the asset by the appointed person are treated as done by the owner.

(e) The legal ownership of the assets of a trust is held by the person or persons who, from time to time, hold the position of trustee of the trust and a change in the person or persons who hold the position of trustee does not, of itself, constitute a disposal of the assets of the trust. § 404. Cost of an asset

(a) The cost of an intangible asset of a taxpayer is not reduced by any impairment write down in relation to the asset made in the taxpayer’s financial accounts because the fair market value of the asset is less than the cost of the asset.

(b) Where the acquisition of an asset by a taxpayer constitutes:

(1) the derivation of an amount included in the gross revenue of the taxpayer, the cost of the asset is the amount so included plus any amount paid by the taxpayer for the asset; or

(2) the derivation of exempt income of the taxpayer, the cost of the asset is the exempt amount plus any amount paid by the taxpayer for the asset. § 405. Cost when an asset has been damaged

(a) Where an asset is damaged, the cost of the asset is reduced by any amount received for the damage under:

(1) an insurance policy, indemnity, or other agreement;

(2) a settlement; or

(3) a judicial decision.

(b) Where Sub-regulation (a) applies and the amount received exceeds the cost of the damaged asset:

(1) the owner of the damaged asset is treated as having made a gain equal to the amount of the excess at the time that the amount is received;

(2) the gain has the same tax treatment under the Act as a gain on disposal of the damaged asset; and

(3) the cost of the damaged asset is set at zero. § 406. Cost of split assets

(a) Where a taxpayer disposes of a part of an asset, the cost of the asset allocated to the part of the asset disposed of is calculated in accordance with the following formula:

A x B /(B + C) where:

A is the cost of the asset;

B is the consideration for the part of the asset disposed of; and C is the fair market value of the retained part of the asset at the time of the disposal.

(b) Where Sub-regulation (a) applies:

(1) the retained part of the asset is treated as a separate asset; and

(2) the cost of the retained part of the asset is the balance of component A of the formula in Sub-regulation (a) remaining after taking account of the amount allocated under Sub-regulation (a) to the disposed part of the asset.

(c) Where the asset to which Sub-regulation (a) applies is a depreciable asset, the reference to the cost of the asset in component A of the formula in Sub-regulation (a) is a reference to the net book value of the asset at the time of disposal of part of the asset. § 407. Cost of merged assets

(a) The following applies where a taxpayer merges two or more assets (referred to as the

“original assets”) into a single asset (referred to as the “merged asset”):

(1) the original assets are treated as having been disposed of at the time of the merger of the assets;

(2) no gain or loss is recognized in respect of the disposal of the original assets referred to in Paragraph (1);

(3) the taxpayer is treated as having acquired the merged asset at the time of the merger of the assets; and

(4) the cost of the merged asset is the total of the following amounts:

(A) the cost of the original assets at the time of the merger of the assets; and

(B) the costs incurred by the taxpayer in merging the original assets into the merged asset.

(b) Where an original asset under Sub-regulation (a) is a depreciable asset, the reference to “cost” in Sub-regulation (a)(4) is a reference to the net book value of the asset at the time of the merger of the assets. § 408. Cost of land and an improvement to land

(a) Where a building or other improvement to land is a depreciable asset, the building or other improvement and the land on which the building or improvement is located are treated as separate assets.

(b) Where the building or other improvement and the land are acquired for a single consideration, the consideration is apportioned as between the cost of the asset that is the building or other improvement and the cost of the asset that is the land.

(c) Apportionment under Sub-regulation (b) is to be undertaken at the time of acquisition of the real property and according to generally accepted valuation principles for land. § 409. Consideration

(a) Where two or more assets are disposed of by a taxpayer in a single transaction and the consideration for each asset is not separately specified, the total consideration is apportioned among the assets disposed of in proportion to their respective fair market values determined at the time of the disposal.

(b) Where a deposit paid to the owner of an asset is wholly or partly forfeited to the owner of the asset because a prospective sale or other disposal of the asset does not proceed:

(1) the owner of the asset is treated as having made a gain equal to the amount of the forfeited deposit at the time that the deposit is forfeited; and

(2) the gain has the same tax treatment under the Act as a gain on disposal of the asset to which the deposit relates.

(c) Where a taxpayer is unable to provide documentary evidence of the consideration for the disposal of an asset by the taxpayer, the consideration is the fair market value of the asset at the time of the disposal. § 410. Grant and exercise of an option

(a) This Regulation applies where a taxpayer (referred to as the “grantor”) grants an option to another person (referred to as the “grantee”) in relation to an asset.

(b) Where this Regulation applies, the following applies to the grantor on the grant of the option:

(1) the grantor is treated as having made a disposal of the option to the grantee at the time that the option is granted;

(2) the cost for the grantor for the option is limited to any non-deductible incidental costs incurred by the grantor in granting the option; and

(3) the consideration for the option is the amount received by the grantor for the option.

(c) Where this Regulation applies, the following applies to the grantee on the grant of an option:

(1) the grantee is treated has having acquired the option at the time that the option is granted; and

(2) the cost for the grantee for the option is the consideration given for the option and any non-deductible incidental costs incurred by the grantee in relation to the grant of the option.

(d) Where the grantee exercises an option:

(1) the grantor’s consideration for the asset transferred by the grantor on exercise of the option does not include the consideration given for the option but only where the grantor has been subject to business profits tax on any income or gain made in respect of the grant of the option; and

(2) the cost for the grantee for the asset transferred on exercise of the option includes the amount specified in Sub-regulation (c)(2).

(e) Where the option expires without being exercised by the grantee, the grantee makes a loss equal to the amount specified in Sub-regulation (c)(2). § 411. Disposal of asset to an associate Subject to Subpart A of Part V, where a taxpayer (referred to as the “transferor”) disposes of an asset to an associate (referred to as the “transferee”):

(1) the transferor is treated as having disposed of the asset for consideration equal to the fair market value of the asset at the time of the disposal; and

(2) the transferee is treated as having acquired the asset for consideration equal to the amount determined under Paragraph (1).

## PART V

International Tax Avoidance Subpart A Transfer Pricing § 501. Application of arm’s length principle

(a) A taxpayer who has entered into a transfer pricing arrangement must determine the income, expenditures, gains, or losses arising under the arrangement in accordance with the arm’s length principle. This requires that the income, expenditures, gains, or losses must be consistent with those reasonably expected to arise under conditions (arm’s length conditions) that apply in a comparable transaction between persons who are not associates dealing wholly independently with each other.

(b) In identifying arm’s length conditions for the purposes of Sub-regulation (a), regard must be had to:

(1) the functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed by the parties to the transfer pricing arrangement;

(2) the characteristics of the property, services, money, intangible, or asset supplied or acquired under the transfer pricing arrangement;

(3) the contractual terms under the transfer pricing arrangement;

(4) the nature of the market in which the transaction has occurred and any other economic factors relating to the transfer pricing arrangement; and

(5) the business strategies of the parties to the transfer pricing arrangement.

(c) Circumstances are comparable to the actual conditions under a transfer pricing arrangement if the circumstances are the same as the actual conditions or, if there are differences, either of the following applies:

(1) the differences are not material; or

(2) reasonably accurate adjustments can be made to eliminate any material differences.

(d) If a taxpayer does not determine the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement in accordance with the arm’s length principle, the Director may make such adjustments as necessary to ensure that the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under the arrangement are in accordance with the arm’s length principle.

(e) The arm’s length principle, including the transfer pricing methods referred to in Regulation 502, is to be applied consistent with international transfer pricing standards applicable to multinational enterprises and tax authorities. Where there is any inconsistency between the Act, these Regulations, and international transfer pricing standards, the Act and these Regulations prevail.

(f) The Director may publish guidelines for the application of the arm’s length principle to particular types of transactions. § 502. Transfer pricing methods

(a) A taxpayer who has entered into a transfer pricing arrangement must determine the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under the arrangement in accordance with the arm’s length principle determined by using the most accurate and reliable transfer pricing method, or combination of transfer pricing methods, having regard to:

(1) the respective strengths and weaknesses of each transfer pricing method in the circumstances of the transfer pricing arrangement;

(2) the accuracy of each transfer pricing method taking account of the nature of the transfer pricing arrangement determined through an analysis of the functions undertaken, assets used, and risks assumed by each party to the arrangement;

(3) the availability of reliable information needed to apply each transfer pricing method; and

(4) the degree of comparability between the conditions under the transfer pricing arrangement and arm’s length conditions, and the reliability of adjustments, if any, that may be required to eliminate differences.

(b) If, having regard to the factors listed in Sub-regulation (a), a taxpayer has used the most accurate and reliable transfer pricing method, the Director’s determination of whether the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement are consistent with the arm's length principle must be based on the transfer pricing method used by the taxpayer.

(c) The transfer pricing methods are:

(1) the comparable uncontrolled price method, which involves comparing the price charged for property transferred or services provided in a controlled transaction with the price charged for property transferred or services provided in a comparable uncontrolled transaction;

(2) the resale price method, which involves comparing the resale margin that a purchaser of property in a controlled transaction earns from reselling that property in an uncontrolled transaction with the resale margin that is earned in comparable uncontrolled purchase and resale transactions;

(3) the cost plus method, which involves comparing the mark up on those costs directly and indirectly incurred in the transfer of property or provision of services in a controlled transaction with the mark up on those costs directly and indirectly incurred in the transfer of property or provision of services in a comparable uncontrolled transaction;

(4) the transaction net margin method, which involves comparing the net profit margin relative to an appropriate base, such as costs, sales, or assets, that a person achieves in a controlled transaction with the net profit margin relative to the same base achieved in comparable uncontrolled transactions; and

(5) the transaction profit split method, which involves allocating to each associate participating in a controlled transaction the portion of common operating profit (or loss) derived from such transaction that an independent person would expect to earn from engaging in a comparable uncontrolled transaction.

(d) A taxpayer may apply a transfer pricing method that is not listed in Sub-regulation (c) if the taxpayer can establish both of the following:

(1) none of the methods in Sub-regulation (a) can reasonably be applied to determine whether the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement is in accordance with the arm’s length principle; and

(2) the method used by the taxpayer gives rise to an outcome that is consistent with the outcome between independent persons dealing under arm’s length conditions.

(e) A controlled transaction is a transaction between associates. A comparable uncontrolled transaction, in relation to a controlled transaction, is a transaction between independent persons that is comparable to the controlled transaction taking account of Regulation 501(c). § 503. Transfer pricing documentation

(a) A taxpayer must have prepared contemporaneous documentation that verifies that the conditions in transactions with associates for a tax year are consistent with the arm’s length principle. The documentation must indicate the basis for using the transfer pricing method or methods. The guidelines referred to in Regulation 501(f) may specify the transfer pricing documents required to be kept by a taxpayer.

(b) Documentation of a taxpayer for a tax year is considered to be contemporaneous where it is prepared by the due date for filing the taxpayer’s business profits tax return for the year.

(c) The Director may provide for simplified transfer pricing documentation for the following:

(1) distributors;

(2) persons with low levels of cross-border transactions;

(3) small and medium enterprises;

(4) low value intra-group services;

(5) loans; and

(6) technical services.

(d) Simplified transfer pricing documentation requirements under Sub-regulation (c) must not apply to royalties, license fees, research and development arrangements, and other intangibles.

(e) A taxpayer who fails to keep documentation as required under this Regulation is liable for a penalty equal to [$50,000].

Subpart B Other International Tax Avoidance Rules § 504. Thin capitalization

(a) This Regulation applies to a foreign-controlled resident company, other than a financial institution. A foreign-controlled resident company is a resident company in which more than 50% of the membership interests in the company are held by a non-resident either alone or together with an associate or associates.

(b) Where a foreign-controlled resident company has an average debt to average equity ratio in excess of 2 to 1 for a tax year, a deduction is disallowed for the interest paid by the company during that year calculated in accordance with the following formula:

A x B /C where:

A is the company’s total amount of deductible interest expenditure for the year ignoring this Regulation;

B is the company’s excess debt for the year. The excess debt of a foreign-controlled resident company for a tax year is the amount by which the company’s average debt for the year exceeds two times the company’s average equity for the year.

C is the company’s average debt for the year.

(c) This Regulation applies to a non-resident company with a permanent establishment in the Republic on the following basis:

(1) the permanent establishment is treated as a foreign-controlled resident company; and

(2) the average debt to average equity ratio of the permanent establishment is calculated by reference to:

(A) the debt obligations of the non-resident company attributable to the permanent establishment; and

(B) the equity of the non-resident company attributable to the operations of the company carried on through the permanent establishment.

(d) The average debt of a foreign-controlled resident company for a tax year is the amount calculated in accordance with the following formula:

A /B where:

A is the sum of the amount of debt of the company at the end of each calendar month in the tax year. The debt of a foreign-controlled resident company is the debt obligations of the company as determined in accordance with GAAP. The reference to debt obligation does not include accounts payable that have been outstanding for less than 120 days.

B is the number of calendar months in the year that the company carried on business in the Republic.

(e) The average equity of a foreign-controlled resident company for a tax year is the amount calculated in accordance with the following formula:

A /B where:

A is the sum of the amount of equity of the company at the end of each calendar month in the tax year. The equity of a foreign-controlled resident company is the equity of the company as determined in accordance with GAAP.

B is the number of calendar months in the year that the company carried on business in the Republic; § 505. Recharged technical fees and royalties

(a) This Regulation applies where the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) a non-resident (referred to as the “supplier”) supplies technical services or leases out equipment, other than through a permanent establishment in the Republic;

(2) the services are supplied, or equipment leased, to a person (referred to as the

“recipient”) who is:

(A) a resident, other than in relation to a business carried on by the resident through a permanent establishment outside the Republic; or

(B) a non-resident carrying on a business in the Republic through a permanent establishment;

(3) the technical fee for the technical services or royalty for the leased equipment is paid to the supplier by a non-resident associate of the recipient; and

(4) the technical fee or royalty is recharged by the associate to the recipient.

(b) Where this Regulation applies, the Act applies as if the non-resident associate is supplying the technical services or leased equipment to the recipient and the recharged amount is the technical fee for the services or royalty for the leased equipment, as the case may be.

## Part VI

Administration of Business Profits Tax § 601. Installments of business profits tax

(a) For the purposes of Section 1464 of the Act, a taxpayer is liable to pay installments of business profits tax for a tax year by the 15th day of the month following the end of the 3rd , 6th , 9th , and 12th months of the year.

(b) Subject to Sub-regulations (c) and (d), the amount of each instalment is calculated according to the following formula: 25% x A where:

A is the taxpayer’s assessed business profits tax liability for the preceding tax year after taking account of any foreign tax credit allowed to the person for that year.

(c) If a taxpayer did not have a business profits tax liability for the previous tax year, the amount of each instalment under Sub-regulation (b) is 2% of that taxpayer’s total gross revenue for the instalment period.

(d) A taxpayer may apply to the Director, in the approved form, for a variation in the amount of each instalment payable by the taxpayer for the current tax year if the taxpayer has reasonable grounds for believing that:

(1) the business profits tax payable by the taxpayer for the current tax year will be less than that payable for the previous tax year; or

(2) the taxpayer will have a nil business profits tax liability or a net loss for the current tax year.

(e) If Sub-regulation (d)(1) applies, the application must include an estimate of the taxpayer’s business profits tax liability for the current year.

(f) If the Director grants an application under Sub-regulation (d), the amount of each instalment (including a nil amount) is to be based on the taxpayer’s estimated business profits tax liability for the current year.

(g) Each instalment of business profits tax paid by a taxpayer for a tax year is allowed as a tax credit against the business profits tax liability of the taxpayer for the year and any excess credit is to be refunded to the taxpayer within thirty (30) days after the taxpayer filed their business profits tax return for the year. § 602. Penalty for under-estimating installments of business profits tax

(a) This Regulation applies when the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) a taxpayer has applied to the Director under Regulation 601(d) for a variation in the installments of business profits tax payable by the taxpayer for a tax year;

(2) the Director has agreed to the vary the installments of business profits tax payable by the taxpayer for the year based on the taxpayer’s estimate of their business profits tax liability for the year; and

(3) the actual business profits tax payable by the taxpayer for the year exceeds the total instalments paid by the taxpayer for the year by more than 20% (the difference is referred to as the “tax shortfall”).

(b) Where this Regulation applies, the taxpayer is liable for a penalty equal to 10% of the tax shortfall.

## Part VII

Transitional Rules § 701. Transitional Rules

(a) Subject to Sub-regulation (c), if a taxpayer acquires a depreciable asset before the commencement date and uses the asset to derive gross revenue on or after that date, the taxpayer must depreciate the asset on the assumption that the Act applied from the acquisition date of the asset.

(b) If a depreciable asset referred to in Sub-regulation (a) is disposed of after the commencement date, the Act applies to the proportional part of any gain or loss on disposal that relates to the use of the asset to derive gross revenue after the application date.

(c) A depreciable asset of a financial institution subject to tax under repealed Section 1203 of Title 40 that was acquired before the commencement date continues to be depreciated under the depreciation rules applicable for the purposes of the repealed Section 1203 of Title 40.

If a depreciable asset to which this Sub-regulation applies is disposed of after the commencement date, the Act applies on the basis that depreciation deductions allowed for the purposes of repealed 1203 of Title 40 are depreciation deductions under the Act.

(d) The cost of a business asset, other than a depreciable asset, acquired by a taxpayer before the commencement date is:

(1) at the election of the taxpayer, the cost of the asset; or

(2) where Paragraph (1) does not apply, the fair market value of the asset at the commencement date.

(e) For a financial institution subject to tax on net income under the repealed Section 1203 of Title 40, a reference in Section 1432(b) of the Act to a previously deducted expenditure, loss, or bad debt includes a reference to expenditure, loss, or bad debt deducted in calculating net income for the purposes of the repealed Section 1432(b) of the Act.

(f) For the purposes of the Act, if the tax year of a company commences on a date other than the commencement date, the period from the commencement date to the start of the first full tax year under the Act is treated as a separate tax year.

(g) If the first tax year of a company under the Act is a separate tax year under Sub- regulation (f), the amount of an installment of business profits tax payable by the company under Regulation 601 for an installment period in that separate tax year is 2% of the company’s total gross revenue for the installment period.
