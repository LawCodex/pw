## PART I

Preliminary These Regulations may be cited as the Business Profits Tax Regulations. § 102. Authority These Regulations are promulgated pursuant to Section 1472 ofthe Act. § 103. Definitions

(a) Unless the context requires otherwise, for the purpose ofthese Regulations:

(1) "Act" means the Business Profits Act, Chapter 14 ofTitle 40.

(2) "Bank" means a "bank" as defined in Section 1002 ofTitle 26 that is licensed to operate in the Republic.

(3) "Commencement date" means 151 January 2023.

(4) "Cross-bordertransaction" means a transaction between:

(A) a resident and a non-resident except where the transaction takes place wholly in the Republic;

(B) two residents where the transaction relates to a business carried on through a permanent establishment outside the Republic by one or both residents; or

(C) two non-residents where the transaction relates to a business carried on through a permanent establishment in the Republic by one ofthe non- residents.

(5) "General insurance business" means the business ofundertaking liability, by way ofinsurance (including reinsurance), in respect ofany loss or damage, including liability to pay damages or compensation, contingent upon the happening ofa specified event, other than a life insurance business.

(6) "Installment period", in relation to a taxpayer for a tax year, means the period ofthree (3) months ending on the last day ofthe third, sixth, ninth, and twelfth months of the year.

(7) "Intangible asset" means:

(A) a copyright, patent, design or model, plan, secret formula or process, trademark, or other like property or right that has a limited useful life and is used wholly or partly to derive gross revenue;

(B) contractual rights (including arising as a result ofa prepayment of expenses) with a limited term, but which exceeds one (1) year, used wholly or partly to derive gross revenue; or

(C) preliminary expenditure.

(8) "Life insurance business" means the business ofissuing a contract of insurance that provides for the payment ofmoney on the death ofa person or on the happening ofa contingency dependent on the termination or continuance ofhuman life.

(9) "Membership interest", in relation to an entity, means a share in a company, an interest in a partnership or trust, or any other ownership interest in an entity.

(10) "Option" means an agreement under which a person has a right to purchase an asset from, or a right to sell an asset to, another person within an agreed period oftime for a specified or determinable price.

(11) "Preliminary expenditure" means expenditure incurred by a person in establishing a business but before the commencement ofbusiness operations, but only where the business is to be carried on wholly and exclusively to derive gross revenue, other than expenditure incurred to acquire tangible personal, or real property, or an intangible asset within paragraphs (A) or (B) ofthe definition of"intangible asset".

(12) "Property income" means:

(A) a dividend, interest; royalty; rent; or annuity; or

(B) a gain on disposal ofan asset giving rise to income referred to in Sub- paragraph (A).

(13) "Straight-line depreciation" means the calculation ofthe amount ofthe depreciation deduction allowed under Section 1002(y)(10) ofTitle 40 for a depreciable asset by applying the straight-line rate specified in Regulation 202 against the cost ofthe asset in accordance with these Regulations.

(14) "Transfer pricing arrangement" means a transaction, or series oftransactions, that satisfies all the following conditions:

(A) the transaction is for the supply or acquisition ofgoods, services, money, intangibles, or an asset;

(B) the transaction is between persons who are associates; and

(C) the transaction is a cross-border transaction.

(b) A term used in these Regulations that is defined in Title 40 has the meaning in Title 40 unless the context requires otherwise. § 104. Exempt income For the avoidance ofdoubt, the treatment ofan amount as exempt income under the Act or these Regulations is limited to the taxpayer who has derived the amount (referred to as the

"recipient") and does not extend to any person receiving a payment from the recipient that has been made wholly or partly made out ofexempt income.

PART2 Depreciation § 201. Depreciation ofdepreciable assets

(a) A taxpayer may determine the depreciation deduction referred to in Section 1002(y)(l0) ofTitle 40 according to the straight-line method.

(b) The depreciation deduction for a depreciable asset for a tax year is calculated by reference to the rate ofdepreciation specified for the asset in Regulation 202.

(c) If a taxpayer uses a depreciable asset in a tax year partly to derive gross revenue and partly for another use, the amount allowed as a deduction under Section 1002(y)(l0) ofTitle 40 is based on the proportion ofthe use ofthe asset that relates to the derivation ofgross revenue.

(d) Ifa depreciable asset is not used by a taxpayer for the whole ofa tax year to derive gross revenue, the amount allowed as a deduction under Section 1002(y)(l0) ofTitle 40, after taking account ofSub-regulation (c), is based on the proportion ofthe year that the taxpayer used the asset to derive gross revenue.

(e) The total deductions allowed, or that would be allowed but for Sub-regulation (c), to a taxpayer in respect ofa depreciable asset for the current tax year and all previous tax years must not exceed the cost ofthe asset.

(f) Ifthe net book value ofa depreciable asset ofa taxpayer at the end ofa tax year is less than $300:

(1) the undepreciated amount ofthe net book value ofthe asset is added to the taxpayer's depreciation deduction for that year but only to the extent that the asset is used to derive gross revenue; and

(2) the asset is treated as fully depreciated. § 202. Depreciation Rates

Part3 Banks and Insurance Companies § 301. Provision for unexpired risks

(a) A resident company carrying on a general insurance business is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the balance ofthe company's provision for unexpired risks as at the end ofthe year. The deduction is limited to insured risks in the Republic.

(b) A non-resident company carrying on a general insurance business through a permanent establishment in the Republic is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the balance of the company's provision for unexpired risks as at the end ofthe tax year. The deduction is limited to insured risks in the Republic accepted by the permanent establishment.

(c) The amount ofthe deduction must not exceed the amount required for the provision underGAAP.

(d) A resident or non-resident company carrying on a general insurance business for a tax year must include in the gross revenue ofthe company for the year the amount allowed as a deduction to the company under Sub-regulation (a) or (b) in the prior tax year. § 302. Taxation oflife insurance companies

(a) A resident company carrying on a life insurance business (referred to as a "life company") is allowed a deduction for a tax year for the following amounts:

(1) the amount ofthe initial reserves established in the financial accounts ofthe company for new life policies issued during the year for individuals in the Republic, but the amount ofthe deduction allowed must not exceed the amount required for the initial reserve under GAAP~ and

(2) the amount ofthe annual additions made in the financial accounts ofthe company during the year to life policy reserves relating to individuals in the Republic, but the amount ofthe deduction allowed must not exceed the amount required for the annual addition to the reserve under GAAP.

(b) Where, during a tax year, a life company cancels a life policy issued by the company in respect ofwhich a deduction has been allowed under Sub-regulation (a), the amount ofthe deducted reserves in relation to the cancelled policy is included in the gross revenue ofthe company for the year.

(c) Where a life company makes claim pay-outs under life policies relating to individuals in the Republic during a tax year:

(1) ifthe total amount ofdeducted reserves in relation to those claim pay-outs exceeds the total amount ofclaim pay-outs made during the year, the excess is included in the gross revenue ofthe company for the year; or

(2) ifthe total amount ofclaim pay-outs made during the year exceeds the total amount ofdeducted reserves in relation to those claim pay-outs, the company is allowed a deduction for the year for the amount ofthe excess. § 303. Loan loss provision ofbanks

(a) A bank is allowed a deduction for a tax year for an amount equal to the addition to the loan loss provision ofthe bank for the year, provided it is calculated in accordance with GAAP.

(b) The deduction under Sub-regulation (a) is limited to the addition to the loan loss provision relating to banking operations carried on in the Republic.

Part4 Tax Accounting § 401. Application ofPart IV This Part applies for the purposes ofSection 1438 ofthe Act. § 402. Tax accounting for inventory

(a) The amount that a taxpayer is allowed as a deduction for a tax year for the cost of inventory is calculated according to the following formula (A+ B)-C where:

A is the taxpayer's opening value ofinventory for the year;

B is the cost ofinventory acquired by the taxpayer during the year; and C is the taxpayer's closing value ofinventory for the year.

(b) The opening value ofa taxpayer's inventory for a tax year is:

(1) the closing value ofthe taxpayer's inventory for the previous tax year; or

(2) ifthe taxpayer commenced business during the year, the cost ofinventory acquired by the taxpayer prior to the commencement ofthe business that is ventured in the business.

(c) The closing value ofa taxpayer's inventory for a tax year is the lower ofcost or net realizable value ofthe taxpayer's inventory on hand at the end ofthe year.

(d) A taxpayer accounting for income tax on a cash basis in accordance with Section 1438(b) ofthe Act can calculate the cost ofinventory that the taxpayer has manufactured or produced under eitherthe direct-cost method or absorption-cost method.

(e) A taxpayer accounting for income tax on an accrual basis calculates the cost of inventory that the taxpayer has manufactured or produced under the absorption-cost method.

(f) Ifparticular items ofa taxpayer's inventory are not readily identifiable, the taxpayer may account for that inventory under the first-in-first out or weighted average method.

(g) Forthe purposes ofthis Regulation, "absorption-cost method", "direct-cost method",

"first-in-first-out method", "netrealizable value", and "weighted average method" have their meanings under GAAP. § 403. Tax accounting for long-term contracts

(a) A taxpayer undertaking a long-term contract must include in gross revenue for each tax year ofthe contractthe percentage ofthe estimated total net income ofthe taxpayer under the contract determined in accordance with the percentage ofcompletion method under GAAP. A long-term contract is a construction or engineering contract that will take more than 12 months to complete.

(b) Sub-regulation (c) applies where the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) at the end ofthe final tax year ofa long-term contract ofa taxpayer, the taxpayer has a final year loss for the contract that the taxpayer is permitted to carry forward under Section 1437 ofthe Act; and

(2) the taxpayer is unable to do so because the taxpayer ceases to carry on business in Palau at the end ofthe contract.

(c) Where the conditions in Sub-regulation (b) are satisfied, the taxpayer may carry the final year loss back to the preceding tax year and the loss is allowed as a deduction in that year.

A taxpayer can carry back a final year loss for a long-term contract for only one tax year.

(d) A taxpayer has a final year loss for a long-term contract where both the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) the total net income estimated to be made by the taxpayer under the contract for the purposes ofthe percentage ofcompletion method exceeds the actual total net income under the contract; and

(2) the amount ofthe excess under Paragraph (1) exceeds the amount to be included in gross revenue under Sub-regulation (a) for the tax year in which the contract was completed.

(e) The amount ofa final year loss is the amount ofthe excess under Sub-regulation

(d)(2). § 404. Tax accounting for finance leases

(a) Where a person (referred to as the "lessor") has leased a business asset to another person (referred to as the "lessee") under a lease that is finance lease under GAAP, the Act applies to the lessor and lessee on the following basis:

(1) the lessee, and not the lessor, is treated as the owner ofthe business asset;

(2) the lessee is treated as having acquired the business asset at the commencement ofthe lease; and

(3) the lessor is treated as having made a loan to the lessee at the commencement ofthe lease and each lease payment is in part repayment ofthe principal under the loan and in part payment ofinterest.

(b) The cost to a lessee ofa leased asset treated as owned by the lessee under Sub- regulation (a)(l) is:

(1) where the lessor and lessee are not associates and an amount is stated as the cost or value ofthe asset in the lease agreement, that amount; or

(2) in any other case, the fair market value ofthe asset at the commencement of the lease.

(c) The amount ofthe loan referred to in Sub-regulation (a)(3) is the amount determined under Sub-regulation (b) as the cost ofthe leased asset.

(d) The interest part ofeach payment made under the loan is calculated by reference to the interest rate implicit in the lease agreement and is treated as interest for the purposes ofthe Act. § 405. Tax accounting and PGST

(a) The gross revenue ofa POST-registered person does not include:

(1) any output tax receivable by the person on a taxable supply made by the person; or

(2) any POST refunded by the Director to the person.

(b) A POST-registered person is not allowed a deduction for input tax payable by the person on a creditable acquisition made by the person to the extent that the person is allowed a credit for the input tax under Chapter 12.

(c) The cost ofan asset acquired by a POST-registered person does not include any input tax payable in respect ofan amount included in the cost ofthe asset under the Act or these Regulations to the extent that the person is allowed a credit for the input tax under Chapter 12.

## Part 5

Rules Relating to Assets § 501. Jointly owned assets

(a) For the purposes ofthe Act, where an asset is jointly owned by two or more persons (other than in pattnership or under a trust), any income, gains, expenditures, and losses relating to the asset are apportioned among the owners in accordance with their respective interests in the asset.

(b) Where the interests ofthe owners ofajointly owned asset cannot be ascertained, the owners ofthe asset are treated as having an equal interest in the asset. § 502. Acquisition ofan asset

(a) Subject to the Act and this Part, a taxpayer acquires an asset at the time that legal title to the asset is transferred to the taxpayer.

(b) A taxpayer acquires a right at the time that the right is granted to the taxpayer.

(c) Where a taxpayer constructs or creates, or arranges for the construction or creation of, an asset that the taxpayer owns when the asset is constructed or created, the taxpayer acquires the asset when the construction or the work that resulted in the creation ofthe asset commences. § 503. Disposal ofan asset

(a) This Regulation provides for the disposal ofan asset in addition to the rules in the Act.

(b) An asset transmitted by succession or under a will is treated as disposed ofby the deceased at the time the asset is transmitted.

(c) A disposal includes the disposal ofa part ofan asset.

(d) The vesting ofan asset ofa taxpayer (referred to as the "owner") in an appointed person refen·ed to in Section 1612 ofTitle 40 is not a disposal ofthe asset for the purposes ofthe Act, and any acts done in relation to the asset by the appointed person are treated as done by the owner.

(e) The legal ownership ofthe assets ofa trust is held by the person or persons who, from time to time, hold the position oftrustee ofthe trust and a change in the person or persons who hold the position oftrustee does not, ofitself, constitute a disposal ofthe assets ofthe trust. § 504. Cost of an asset

(a) The cost ofan intangible asset ofa taxpayer is not reduced by any impairment write down in relation to the asset made in the taxpayer's financial accounts because the fair market value ofthe asset is less than the cost ofthe asset.

(b) Where the acquisition ofan asset by a taxpayer constitutes:

(1) the derivation ofan amount included in the gross revenue ofthe taxpayer, the cost ofthe asset is the amount so included plus any amount paid by the taxpayer for the asset; or

(2) the derivation ofexempt income ofthe taxpayer, the cost ofthe asset is the exempt amount plus any amount paid by the taxpayer for the asset. §505. Cost when an asset has been damaged

(a) Where an asset is damaged, the cost ofthe asset is reduced by any amount received for the damage under:

(1) an insurance policy, indemnity, or other agreement;

(2) a settlement; or

(3) a judicial decision.

(b) Where Sub-regulation (a) applies, and the amount received exceeds the cost ofthe damaged asset:

(1) the owner ofthe damaged asset is treated as having made a gain equal to the amount ofthe excess at the time that the amount is received;

(2) the gain has the same tax treatment under the Act as a gain on disposal ofthe damaged asset; and

(3) the cost ofthe damaged asset is set at zero. §506. Cost ofsplit assets

(a) Where a taxpayer disposes ofa part ofan asset, the cost ofthe asset allocated to the part ofthe asset disposed ofis calculated in accordance with the following formula:

(a) The following applies where a taxpayer merges two or more assets (referred to as the

"original assets") into a single asset (referred to as the "merged asset"):

(1) the original assets are treated as having been disposed ofat the time ofthe merger ofthe assets;

(2) no gain or loss is recognized in respect ofthe disposal ofthe original assets referred to in Paragraph (1);

(3) the taxpayer is treated as having acquired the merged asset at the time ofthe merger ofthe assets; and

(4) the cost ofthe merged asset is the total ofthe following amounts:

(A) the cost ofthe original assets at the time ofthe merger ofthe assets; and

(B) the costs incurred by the taxpayer in merging the original assets into the merged asset.

(b) Where an original asset under Sub-regulation (a) is a depreciable asset, the reference to "cost" in Sub-regulation (a)(4) is a reference to the net book value ofthe asset at the time of the merger ofthe assets. §508. Cost of land and an improvement to land

(a) Where a building or other improvement to land is a depreciable asset, the building or other improvement and the land on which the building or improvement is located are treated as separate assets.

(b) Where the building or other improvement and the land are acquired for a single consideration, the consideration is apportioned as between the cost ofthe asset that is the building or other improvement and the cost ofthe asset that is the land.

(c) Apportionment under Sub-regulation (b) is to be undertaken at the time ofacquisition ofthe real property and according to generally accepted valuation principles for land. §509. Consideration

(a) Where two or more assets are disposed ofby a taxpayer in a single transaction and the consideration for each asset is not separately specified, the total consideration is apportioned among the assets disposed ofin proportion to their respective fair market values determined at the time ofthe disposal.

(b) Where a deposit paid to the owner ofan asset is wholly or partly forfeited to the owner ofthe asset because a prospective sale or other disposal ofthe asset does not proceed:

(1) the owner ofthe asset is treated as having made a gain equal to the amount of the forfeited deposit at the time that the deposit is forfeited; and

(2) the gain has the same tax treatment under the Act as a gain on disposal ofthe asset to which the deposit relates.

(c) Where a taxpayer is unable to provide documentary evidence ofthe consideration for the disposal ofan asset by the taxpayer, the consideration is the fair market value ofthe asset at the time ofthe disposal. § 510. Grant and exercise ofan option

(a) This Regulation applies where a taxpayer (referred to as the "grantor") grants an option to another person (referred to as the "grantee") in relation to an asset.

(b) Where this Regulation applies, the following applies to the grantor on the grant ofthe option:

(1) the grantor is treated as having made a disposal ofthe option to the grantee at the time that the option is granted;

(2) the cost for the grantor for the option is limited to any non-deductible incidental costs incurred by the grantor in granting the option; and

(3) the consideration for the option is the amount received by the grantor for the option.

(c) Where this Regulation applies, the following applies to the grantee on the grant ofan option:

(1) the grantee is treated has having acquired the option at the time that the option is granted; and

(2) the cost for the grantee for the option is the consideration given for the option and any non-deductible incidental costs incurred by the grantee in relation to the grant of the option.

(d) Where the grantee exercises an option:

(1) the grantor's consideration for the asset transferred by the grantor on exercise ofthe option does not include the consideration given for the option but only where the grantor has been subject to business profits tax on any income or gain made in respect of the grant ofthe option; and

(2) the cost for the grantee for the asset transferred on exercise ofthe option includes the amount specified in Sub-regulation (c)(2).

(e) Where the option expires without being exercised by the grantee, the grantee makes a loss equal to the amount specified in Sub-regulation (c)(2). § 511. Disposal ofasset to an associate Subjectto Subpart A ofPart V, where a taxpayer (referred to as the "transferor") disposes ofan asset to an associate (referred to as the "transferee"):

(1) the transferor is treated as having disposed ofthe asset for consideration equal to the fair market value ofthe asset at the time ofthe disposal; and

(2) the transferee is treated as having acquired the asset for consideration equal to the amount determined under Paragraph (1).

PART6 International Tax Avoidance Subpart A Transfer Pricing § 601. Application ofarm's length principle

(a) A taxpayer who has entered into a transfer pricing arrangement must determine the income, expenditures, gains, or losses arising under the arrangement in accordance with the arm's length principle. This requires that the income, expenditures, gains, or losses must be consistent with those reasonably expected to arise under conditions (arm's length conditions) that apply in a comparable transaction between persons who are not associates dealing wholly independently with each other.

(b) In identifying arm's length conditions for the purposes ofSub-regulation (a), regard must be had to:

(1) the functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed by the parties to the transfer pricing arrangement;

(2) the characteristics ofthe property, services, money, intangible, or asset supplied or acquired under the transfer pricing arrangement;

(3) the contractual terms under the transfer pricing arrangement;

(4) the nature ofthe market in which the transaction has occurred and any other economic factors relating to the transfer pricing arrangement; and

(5) the business strategies ofthe parties to the transfer pricing arrangement.

(c) Circumstances are comparable to the actual conditions under a transfer pricing arrangement ifthe circumstances are the same as the actual conditions or, ifthere are differences, either ofthe following applies:

(1) the differences are not material; or

(2) reasonably accurate adjustments can be made to eliminate any material differences.

(d) Ifa taxpayer does not determine the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement in accordance with the arm's length principle, the Director may make such adjustments as necessary to ensure that the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under the arrangement are in accordance with the arm's length principle.

(e) The arm's length principle, including the transfer pricing methods referred to in Regulation 602, is to be applied consistent with international transfer pricing standards applicable to multinational enterprises and tax authorities. Where there is any inconsistency between the Act, these Regulations, and international transfer pricing standards, the Act and these Regulations prevail.

(f) The Director may publish guidelines for the application ofthe arm's length principle to particular types oftransactions. § 602. Transfer pricing methods

(a) A taxpayer who has entered into a transfer pricing arrangement must determine the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under the arrangement in accordance with the arm's length principle determined by using the most accurate and reliable transfer pricing method, or combination oftransfer pricing methods, having regard to:

(1) the respective strengths and weaknesses ofeach transfer pricing method in the circumstances ofthe transfer pricing arrangement;

(2) the accuracy ofeach transfer pricing method taking account ofthe nature of the transfer pricing arrangement determined through an analysis ofthe functions undertaken, assets used, and risks assumed by each party to the arrangement;

(3) the availability ofreliable information needed to apply each transfer pricing method; and

(4) the degree ofcomparability between the conditions under the transfer pricing arrangement and arm's length conditions, and the reliability ofadjustments, ifany, that may be required to eliminate differences.

(b) If, having regard to the factors listed in Sub-regulation (a), a taxpayer has used the most accurate and reliable transfer pricing method, the Director's determination ofwhether the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement are consistent with the arm's length principle must be based on the transfer pricing method used by the taxpayer.

(c) The transfer pricing methods are:

(1) the comparable uncontrolled price method, which involves comparing the price charged for property transferred or services provided in a controlled transaction with the price charged for property transferred or services provided in a comparable uncontrolled transaction;

(2) the resale price method, which involves comparing the resale margin that a purchaser ofproperty in a controlled transaction earns from reselling that property in an uncontrolled transaction with the resale margin that is earned in comparable uncontrolled purchase and resale transactions;

(3) the cost-plus method, which involves comparing the mark up on those costs directly and indirectly incurred in the transfer ofproperty or provision ofservices in a controlled transaction with the mark up on those costs directly and indirectly incurred in the transfer ofproperty or provision ofservices in a comparable uncontrolled transaction;

(4) the transaction net margin method, which involves comparing the net profit margin relative to an appropriate base, such as costs, sales, or assets, that a person achieves in a controlled transaction with the net profit margin relative to the same base achieved in comparable uncontrolled transactions; and

(5) the transaction profit split method, which involves allocating to each associate participating in a controlled transaction the portion ofcommon operating profit (or loss) derived from such transaction that an independent person would expect to earn from engaging in a comparable uncontrolled transaction.

(d) A taxpayer may apply a transfer pricing method that is not listed in Sub-regulation (c) ifthe taxpayer can establish both ofthe following:

(1) none ofthe methods in Sub-regulation (a) can reasonably be applied to determine whether the income, expenditures, gains, and losses arising under a transfer pricing arrangement is in accordance with the arm's length principle; and

(2) the method used by the taxpayer gives rise to an outcome that is consistent with the outcome between independent persons dealing under arm's length conditions.

(e) A controlled transaction is a transaction between associates. A comparable uncontrolled transaction, in relation to a controlled transaction, is a transaction between independent persons that is comparable to the controlled transaction taking account of Regulation 601(c). § 603. Transfer pricing documentation

(a) A taxpayer must have prepared contemporaneous documentation that verifies that the conditions in transactions with associates for a tax year are consistent with the arm's length principle. The documentation must indicate the basis for using the transfer pricing method or methods. The guidelines referred to in Regulation 601(f) may specify the transfer pricing documents required to be kept by a taxpayer.

(b) Documentation of a taxpayer for a tax year is considered to be contemporaneous where it is prepared by the due date for filing the taxpayer's business profits tax return for the year.

(c) The Director may provide for simplified transfer pricing documentation for the following:

(1) distributors;

(2) persons with low levels ofcross-border transactions;

(3) small and medium enterprises;

(4) low value intra-group services;

(5) loans; and

(c) Ifa taxpayer did not have a business profits tax liability for the previous tax year, the amount ofeach instalment under Sub-regulation (b) is 2% ofthat taxpayer's total gross revenue for the instalment period.

Penalty for under-estimating installments ofbusiness profits tax

(a) This Regulation applies when the following conditions are satisfied:
